Australian drought, 1900–2025

Dry Country

Australia has always had droughts. What has changed is that they now arrive with heat.

By Aravindh Anand for FIT3179 Data Visualisation Assignment 2

How to read the colours

  • Browns: drier than normal
  • Yellow: close to normal (95–105%)
  • Blue-greens: wetter than normal
  • Reds: hotter than normal
  • "Normal" always means the 1961–1990 average

Part 1 · When

A century of dry spells

Australia's rain swings from year to year and from decade to decade. Four droughts stand out in the record: the Federation, World War II, Millennium and Tinderbox droughts.

How to use these chartsDrag across the top chart to pick a period. The heatmap and timeline below zoom to those years, and the notes above them name the driest year and the longest drought in your selection. Double-click to reset.

South Australia once went 17 years in a row with below-average rain, from 1922 to 1938.

Part 2 · Where

Every drought leaves a different footprint

No two droughts hit the same places. The Millennium Drought was a southern one: Victoria averaged 84% of normal rain for 13 years while Western Australia had 123%. The Tinderbox Drought was centred on New South Wales, which averaged 62%.

Drought doesn't stop at state borders

State averages hide a lot. In the grid of squares, the darkest browns form one block across inland New South Wales, southern Queensland, eastern South Australia and the southern Northern Territory.

The driest spot is in far north-west New South Wales, near Cameron Corner, where it meets Queensland and South Australia. It received only about 20% of its normal rain.

At the same time, much of inland Western Australia was wetter than normal, and parts of the Great Victoria Desert got double their usual rain. The Top End and far north Queensland stayed close to normal.

Over those three years, half of Australia's land received less than 80% of its normal rain, and more than a fifth received less than 60%.

Part 3 · How often and how long

Some states dry out far more often

Since 1900, South Australia has had 40 dry years (under 80% of normal rain). Tasmania has had six. Three states recorded their driest year on record in 2019.

The difference is how far each state's rain swings. South Australia and the Northern Territory regularly fall well below normal and rise well above it; Tasmania's rain barely moves.

Hover over a 2019 dot and six of the seven states light up as dry.

Part 4 · Heat

Droughts are getting hotter

Rain tells only half the story. Australia as a whole hasn't dried out since 1900, but since the 1970s every decade has been warmer than the one before.

In the 1950s, about 1 in 70 state-years was hot. In the 2010s, 49 in 70 were.

Dry years, meanwhile, have stayed between about one in seven and one in four in every full decade. Droughts aren't becoming more frequent, but each one now comes with more heat.

What this means

Drought is normal here. Hot drought is the new part.

The record points to three lessons for anyone who farms, manages water or plans for the future.

  1. Expect long droughts

    Every state has had runs of three or more below-average years, and two runs have lasted more than a decade. Planning for one bad year isn't enough.

  2. Location matters as much as severity

    The same years can bring drought to the south-east and above-average rain to the north, so national averages can hide a crisis.

  3. Heat raises the stakes

    In the Tinderbox Drought, six of seven states were at least 1 °C hotter than normal. The same shortfall in rain now does more damage.

The record in four numbers

45% of its normal rain fell on New South Wales in 2019, its driest year since 1900
13 years of below-average rain in a row in Victoria during the Millennium Drought
6 of 7 states had a dry year in 2019, at the height of the Tinderbox Drought
+1.7 °C above normal: New South Wales's average temperature during the Tinderbox Drought

Notes on method

  • "Normal" is the 1961–1990 average for each state, the Bureau's standard reference period.
  • A dry year has less than 80% of normal rain. A drought run is three or more below-average years in a row.
  • The ACT is included in New South Wales, as in the Bureau's data.
